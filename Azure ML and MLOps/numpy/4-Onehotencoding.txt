Models only understand numbers, not text labels like "low/medium/high." One-hot encoding turns each category into its own 0/1 column,
so no false numeric order is implied (unlike labeling low=0, medium=1, high=2, which wrongly suggests high is "more" than low mathematically).


In simple terms:

- np.unique(categories) finds the different category names and sorts them.

- np.searchsorted(...) gives each category its position number.

- np.eye(...) creates an identity matrix, and indexing it makes one-hot encoding.

What we can achieve?

- Turn words into a machine-friendly format.

- Represent each category as a separate binary column.

- Avoid giving categories a fake order or numeric meaning.

For example, medium becomes something like [0, 0, 1], while low becomes [0, 1, 0].

Output:

Categories: ['high' 'low' 'medium']
One-hot matrix:
 [[0. 1. 0.]
 [0. 0. 1.]
 [1. 0. 0.]
 [0. 0. 1.]
 [0. 1. 0.]]
