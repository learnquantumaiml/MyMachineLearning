- Accuracy alone can be misleading (e.g., a model predicting "no disease" 95% of the time looks 95% accurate on rare-disease data, but it's useless). 
- Precision, recall, and F1 give a fuller picture.

What it does, simply:

- tp, tn, fp, and fn count correct and wrong predictions.
- precision tells you: when the model predicts “positive,” how often is it right.
- recall tells you: out of all real positives, how many the model found.
- F1 gives one score that balances precision and recall.

What we can achieve with this:

- Judge model performance beyond just accuracy.
- Understand whether the model is making too many false alarms or missing too many positives.
- Compare different models in a clearer way, especially for imbalanced datasets.

Example: if you care about catching fraud or disease, recall is very important; if false alarms are costly, 
         precision matters more.
